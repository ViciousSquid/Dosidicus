# Multiplayer: how squid live together

Players enable the possibility of encounters. Squid make their own decisions.
Evolution emerges from the consequences.

| Document | What it covers |
|---|---|
| [The social network](social-network.md) | How another squid reaches the brain (vision, closeness, memory), and the small network of social senses and neurons that grows inside the squid's own network - what grows, when, how it is wired, how it can change for good |
| [Social learning](social-learning.md) | How encounters change a squid: what it remembers of individuals and what it forgets, how it recognises them, how every encounter moves how it meets strangers, and why two squid born alike diverge |
| [Fights](fights.md) | Predisposition (the grown aggression neuron) versus ability (body, experience, commitment), what a fight costs, and the record kept of every one |
| [Rocks](rocks.md) | Throw or drop from the network, aim and speed, swept hits that are only felt, and how impacts cross the wire safely |
| [Reading a squid's social mind](reading-the-mind.md) | Where to read what a squid knows of other squid, apart from its tank: the Memory tab's Social page, the Laboratory's social cards, and the Decisions tab's social thoughts |
| [Mating, eggs and inheritance](reproduction.md) | How a mating happens, the parent and its egg, and how a hatchling inherits half its weights from each parent |

The rules all of it follows:

- **Nothing is scripted.** No code decides what a squid does about another;
  its senses report, its network responds, the decision engine reads the
  winner. Personality enters only as wiring.
- **Nothing for other squid is innate.** Every social sense and neuron grows
  from an encounter; a squid that never meets another has none.
- **Everything has provenance.** Why a neuron exists, why a synapse moved, why
  a fight started and ended as it did - all recorded, all readable.
- **Consequences persist.** Saves are permanently changed; some changes, like
  a raised resting aggression, can never go back.

The plugin's own README (`../README`) covers the protocol, consent, timings and
running two instances.
