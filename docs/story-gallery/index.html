A life across two tanks

Two copies of the desktop game, networked over the multiplayer plugin’s LAN protocol, and two copies of the Android app linked for a visit, captured as they ran. One squid notices another tank, goes there, fights, mates, and dies. Its egg then starts a new game. Every squid here is moved by its own network, and every number and line of thought is from the run.

1Ziggy becomes aware of another tank

Inky’s game comes up on the same network. Ziggy had no way to sense another aquarium, so its brain grew one: aquarium_nearby, the first neuron on a SOCIAL band that did not exist a moment before. Nothing is pre-wired; social senses grow only from encounters.

Ziggy’s own account

  • It sensed another aquarium nearby. I had no way to sense that, so I grew aquarium_nearby: the binary concept of "there is another aquarium nearby".

2It decides to visit

No rule sends it. With curiosity high, Ziggy’s own network takes it to the edge of its tank and out. Its body crosses into Inky’s tank, but its brain keeps running at home. Inky’s game sends it what its body perceives, and it sends back what it means to do.

Ziggy’s own account

  • Another aquarium is nearby and I want to explore it: I am leaving through the left side.

⇄Two desktop games, networked

The same moment in two separate desktop processes, talking over UDP multicast, the same as two PCs on a LAN. On the left, Ziggy’s home tank stands empty while Ziggy is away. On the right, Ziggy’s body enters Inky’s tank, driven from the other process.

3They get into a fight

Inky is holding a rock. When Ziggy comes close, the rock between them flashes dark red: it is what they are fighting over. Then the scuffle. Who starts it is each squid’s aggression, and who wins comes from each one’s ability, personality, experience and condition, never a script.

Inky’s own account

  • I went for Ziggy: my aggression was 85, past the 50 it takes to start a fight.
  • I won: my ability 135 against their 50 gave me a 88% chance.

Ziggy’s own account

  • Inky came at me. I fought back with my defence at 0.
  • I lost: my ability 50 against their 135 gave me a 12% chance.

Inky’s game saysInky: Won a fight it started (aggression 85): ability 135 against 50, so it had a 88% chance.

4They mate

Still in contact after the fight, the two mate: one heart beats over the pair. Inky lays an egg and, as a parent, stops eating. The egg holds both parents’ weights; Ziggy’s came over the network as a gamete.

Inky’s game saysInky and Ziggy produced an egg. Inky has stopped eating.

Ziggy goes home, changed

Tired, Ziggy goes home to sleep, by its own rule. It comes back with a social network it did not have before the visit:

  • I am tired: I went home to sleep.

5Inky dies

A parent keeps its egg and will not eat. Inky starves, and a headstone marks the spot, beside the egg. Hovering over the headstone shows the epitaph:

RIP Inky
Lived 1m 16s
Fights won: 1, lost: 0

6A new game starts

The egg hatches, and that is a new game. There is no way out, just as a game starts with no save. The hatchling begins with a newborn brain overlaid with half of each parent’s weights: 24 synapses inherited from Inky and Ziggy, each recorded in the brain’s ledger with the parent it came from. It has no memories, and its parent’s headstone stays in the tank.

▣An Android multiplayer session

Two copies of the Android app (its Kivy UI and engine, phone-sized), each in its own process. Pip hosts, and Nova finds Pip and connects. Nova’s body swims in Pip’s tank while its brain stays on Nova’s phone, which says so. Pip, spoiling for a fight, scuffles with it, and Pip’s brain grows its own SOCIAL band:

can_see_squidsocial_fightsocial_playsquid_nearby

Google Nearby needs two phones with real radios, so for this capture a loopback socket stands in for it. It delivers the same events in the same order (peer found, connection initiated, accepted, connected, bytes) behind the same NearbyManager calls. Everything above the transport is the app as shipped.

What was nudged

A lifetime has to fit in a few minutes, and the scenes have to happen while the camera is on. So the capture scripts set some drives and timings and log each change. They never choose an action for a squid. Every nudge, as logged:

  1. Ziggy kept playful and unafraid while visiting (act_play 85, flee/rest/shelter 0, anxiety 30, fed and rested)
  2. A night compressed: Ziggy's sleepiness set to 100 once it had mated, so it goes home to sleep by its own rule
  3. Ziggy's player switched multiplayer off once Ziggy was home, so the story can follow Inky
  4. A rock put in Inky’s tentacles and held (carry time raised to 60 s)
  5. Inky placed mid-tank and held still, facing Ziggy, while the two are close
  6. Inky alarmed and aggressive for the scene (anxiety 75, aggression 85)
  7. Mating chance (2% per visit) made certain after the fight
  8. Starvation compressed: Inky’s hunger set to 100 and health to 1.5 (a parent loses 0.1 health a second once starving)
  9. Android: Pip's aggression raised to 85 so the two scuffle
  10. Android: Nova kept playful and unafraid while visiting (act_play 85, flee/rest 0, anxiety 25)

How these were made

  • Desktop: tools/capture_story.py runs two real game processes (offscreen Qt) with the multiplayer plugin on its multicast protocol, and screenshots each window.
  • Android: tools/capture_android.py runs the Kivy app twice under a virtual display and presses the buttons a player would: Host on one phone, Find and Connect on the other.
  • The game messages and thoughts quoted here are copied from each run’s log, not written for the page: docs/story-gallery/source/build.py builds the page from the two runs’ JSON.
